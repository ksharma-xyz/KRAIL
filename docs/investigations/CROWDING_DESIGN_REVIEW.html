Part 2Riders and wordingWho looks, where to stand, the next service, night, trust, the writing guide

Who is looking, and what they need

Rushing commuter

Glances for a second while walking. Wants to know where to stand, nothing more.Risk: anything longer than a short line is ignored.

Needs a seat

Older riders, pregnancy, injury, a child on the lap. A seat matters more than a few minutes.Risk: we mention a later train and it is full too.

Travelling at night

Wants to feel safe. An empty carriage at 11pm is not a feature.Risk: pointing someone to the emptiest car after dark.

Visitor

Does not know the lines. Plain words, nothing to decode.Risk: car numbers and line codes mean nothing.

Tight connection

Has a change to make. A later train can break the trip.Risk: mentioning a later train that misses the connection.

Front, middle, back. Never car numbers

Nobody counts carriages on a platform. Three zones are enough to act on, and the back can be explained by something a rider sees: the end the train comes in from.

Zones only. Each is the busiest level among its cars, so room is never overstated.
Short trains stop mid-platform, so the helper says so instead of pointing at a platform end.
The zone must be right, or it is worse than nothing. Must verifyCarriage order differs between feeds and flips when a train reverses.

The next service: inform, never tell anyone to skip

If KRAIL says "skip this one" and the next is full too, KRAIL gets the blame. So the app states what it sees, with times, and the rider decides.

Only when it is clearly better: at least one full level quieter.
Only when it is close: leaves within about 10 minutes of this one.
Name it the way the station board does: line, clock time with am or pm, and platform. Tapping the line jumps to that journey's card.
Never with a tight connection later in the trip, the last service of the night, or a long gap after it.
Always with its arrival time. One suggestion at most.

Night: quiet is not the goal

NSW Police advise waiting in the Nightsafe area marked in blue on the platform, so you board the carriage closest to the guard (NSW Police). After about 9pm KRAIL says that instead of zone advice, and never mentions a later train.

Writing the advice

Full sentences with a verb. No fragments like "At Central 9:26". Read it aloud.
Cheap to be wrong and the rider can check: speak to the rider."Seats at the back of the train." "Wait in the blue Nightsafe area."
Costs time if wrong: the service gets the verb, the rider keeps the choice."Want a seat? The next T1, at 8:59am from Platform 1, has seats available and is due at Central at 9:26am." Never skip, wait or don't board.
Goal, then fact, then cost. Certainty in one word: "has" live, "should" forecast, "usually" proven, never "will".
Two sentences at most, about twelve words each. Stations and clock times riders recognise.

Peak results

Monday evening peak below. Tuesday morning peak lands here at about 9:55am. The collector stopped between 10:14am and 5:00pm on Monday, so the evening sample covers 5:00 to 6:30pm, the busiest part of the peak.

What the evening says about each rider line

Supports "Seats at both ends of the train." When the cars differ, an end third had more room than the middle on 36 to 61% of readings by line, and the middle beat both ends on only 1 to 11% (T8 Airport highest, n 760 mixed readings).
Supports, blocked "Seats at the back" or "at the front". On each line one end wins almost every time in feed order: Metro all one end (n 1,476), T2 Inner West 336 to 59, North Shore 156 to 23, Blue Mountains 157 to 7. Which real end that is stays unknown until the platform check.
Weakens how often zone advice applies. Only 19 to 37% of suburban readings had mixed cars; Metro and Blue Mountains about 60%. Most of the time the honest headline is "Seats available all along the train" or "Standing only, all along the train".
Supports forecast wording with "expected". Within one level 93 to 96% of the time at every lead time (n 17,095 to 24,580), exactly right 62 to 68%, lower than Monday morning's 71 to 77%. Never promise a seat.
Kills, for now "It usually eases off after a named station". No station passed 6 trips in one evening. Needs the many-day collection before any station is named.
Weakens "The next bus has seats available". Buses were 90% Seats available (n 75,336). Of close bus pairs, one was roomier 18% of the time and Standing against Seats only 3% (n 12,809 pairs).
Weakens a light rail chip. Parramatta was all Seats available (n 255), Newcastle 97% (n 87). L1 to L3 and ferries report nothing.
Lead where crowding is real: Metro M1 was Standing only on 40% of readings (n 2,746). Central Coast 73% (n 37) and South Coast toward Wollongong 100% (n 16) are too few readings to lean on.

Part 4DecisionsAll decided, each with a rider story and the trade-offs

Decisions

All six are decided (26 October), code 1A 2A 3A 4A 5C 6A. Each card tells a short rider story, then what the rider sees with each option and what it costs. The decided option is tagged. Click another to see the answer code change.

Wording, also decided: say "seats", never "room", and match the level ("Seats at the back of the train", "A few seats left at the back"). Name any other service with its clock time and platform, and its arrival as "due at".

Before any build
  1. Which end is the front: car order differs between feeds and flips when a train reverses. Check on a real platform before any zone advice ships.
  2. Use the level at the rider's boarding stop.
  3. When carriage data exists, the chip is worked out from the cars, so the two never disagree.
